# Key message
The benefit of digitization depends on the quality of the data that is digitized. Coverage figures describe how much has been converted, not how accurate the converted data is.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Why quality changes outcomes
Where textual records and survey maps disagree on area, ownership or survey numbers, digitizing both without reconciliation preserves the disagreement in a form that is harder to notice. Offices in such settings reported that new errors surfaced as citizens accessed records online, which increased correction requests in the first months. Reconciliation before go-live lowered this surge.

## Recommended practices
Programs that pair coverage targets with a data-quality factor, such as the share of records passing automated consistency checks, make progress easier to interpret. A quality adjustment prevents high coverage from being read as high reliability. Public audit trails allow errors to be traced and corrected without erasing history.
