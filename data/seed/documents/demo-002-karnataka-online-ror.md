# Background
This illustrative case study describes a state where the record-of-rights was moved from manual registers to an online system with an application tracker. It is written to demonstrate how case-study evidence is stored and cited on the platform.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Implementation
The rollout proceeded district by district, beginning with districts where textual records were already partly computerized. Field offices received training on the tracker and on correcting mismatches between the register and the survey sketch. A statewide helpdesk handled citizen queries during the first year.

## Observed changes in processing
Officials in the illustrative case reported that routine mutation requests moved through fewer manual hand-offs after the tracker was introduced. Processing time for uncontested mutations fell markedly, while contested mutations continued to require hearings and remained slow. Backlog in offices with few trained staff declined more slowly than in well-staffed offices.

<!-- page: 3 -->
## Disputes and grievances
Interviewees in the case described fewer grievances about missing or altered register entries. They did not observe a comparable decline in boundary disputes, which they attributed to survey sketches that lagged behind the online textual record. The case study cautions that the timing of the rollout overlapped with a survey update drive, so the two effects cannot be separated.

## Lessons
Sequencing matters: cleaning and reconciling records before digitization avoided the reconciliation backlog seen in offices that digitized first and corrected later. Sustained training and a visible audit trail were repeatedly mentioned as the features that built citizen trust.
