# Executive summary
Digitization of land records can reduce administrative friction in mutation, certified-copy issue and registration follow-up. The synthesis finds consistent evidence for faster routine processing where records are complete and reconciled, and more mixed evidence for reductions in land disputes.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Effects on administrative processing
Studies reviewed in this synthesis report shorter turnaround times for routine mutation requests after online record systems were introduced, particularly where applicant-facing counters were replaced by tracked online workflows. The gain is largest when the underlying textual records were cleaned before digitization. Where digitization simply copied inconsistent registers, offices reported additional reconciliation work that offset part of the time saved.

<!-- page: 3 -->
## Effects on land disputes
Evidence that digitization reduces land disputes is suggestive rather than conclusive. Several case studies describe fewer disputes rooted in lost, altered or duplicated paper entries after digitization. However, disputes arising from unclear boundaries, competing inheritance claims and unrecorded transactions are not resolved by digitization alone. Reported declines in dispute filings often coincide with concurrent reforms such as survey updates and grievance redressal, which makes attribution difficult.

## Conditions that appear to matter
Four conditions recur across the reviewed cases: reconciliation of textual and spatial records before go-live, sustained staff training, a public and auditable change history for each record, and an accessible correction procedure for citizens. Digitization coverage on its own is a weak predictor of dispute outcomes when these conditions are absent.

<!-- page: 4 -->
## Implications for scenario analysis
For planning purposes, coverage expansion is best treated as one input alongside implementation capacity and data quality. Scenario tools should therefore expose capacity and quality assumptions explicitly, and should present modeled results as illustrative estimates rather than forecasts of dispute reduction.
