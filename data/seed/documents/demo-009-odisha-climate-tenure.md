# Abstract
This illustrative paper explores how climate exposure and land tenure security interact in coastal districts. It is a demonstration document and does not report real survey data.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Climate exposure and records
After cyclones and floods, households without documented tenure face greater difficulty proving their claim to land and accessing relief and reconstruction support. Loss or damage of paper records in flood-affected offices delayed verification. Districts with digitized and backed-up records were described as recovering documentation faster.

## Land-use pressure
Coastal erosion and salinity ingress reduce cultivable area and can trigger informal transfers and boundary changes that are not recorded. These changes widen the gap between records and reality and increase the risk of disputes when land is re-allocated or compensated.

<!-- page: 3 -->
## Policy relevance
The paper argues that climate-resilient record systems, meaning digitized, geo-referenced and backed up outside the flood zone, are a precondition for fair post-disaster land administration.
