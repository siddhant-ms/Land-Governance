# Abstract
This illustrative paper sets out how the effect of record digitization on land litigation could be estimated and why existing comparisons are hard to interpret causally. It is a demonstration document and reports no real estimates.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Identification strategy
A staggered rollout of digitization across districts allows a difference-in-differences comparison between early and late adopters. The key assumption is that litigation trends in early and late districts would have moved in parallel without digitization. Rollout order is rarely random, because administratively stronger districts are often digitized first.

## Threats to validity
Concurrent reforms such as resurvey, grievance redressal reforms and court-side digitization can produce changes in litigation that are wrongly attributed to record digitization. Filing behaviour also changes: better records can raise the number of recorded disputes in the short run because previously informal claims become visible. Measurement of dispute outcomes differs across courts and revenue tribunals.

<!-- page: 3 -->
## Interpretation
Reported associations between higher digitization coverage and fewer pending disputes should be read as associational unless the design addresses selection and concurrent reforms. Dispute pendency and dispute incidence are different outcomes, and digitization may affect them in different directions and on different time horizons.
