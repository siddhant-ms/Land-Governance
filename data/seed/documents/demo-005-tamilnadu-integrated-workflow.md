# Summary
This illustrative case examines integration of the registration system with the land-record system so that a registered transaction automatically generates a mutation request. It exists to demonstrate multi-state coverage in the demo corpus.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## What changed
Integration removed a manual data-entry step between registration offices and revenue offices. Officials reported fewer transcription errors in owner names and survey numbers, which had previously generated correction requests and follow-up visits.

## Capacity findings
Offices with dedicated data-quality staff resolved integration mismatches in days, while offices without such staff accumulated unresolved mismatches. The case concludes that implementation capacity determines how much of the theoretical efficiency gain is realized in practice.

<!-- page: 3 -->
## Limits of the evidence
The case relies on official interviews and process data from a limited set of offices. It does not quantify effects on disputes and should not be generalized beyond similarly resourced districts.
